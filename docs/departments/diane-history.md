### Recurring formats seen across years
1. **Italian-American comfort food made easier**: baked ziti (2019), linguine, tortellini vodka, paccheri with burrata, sheet-pan lasagna, Chicken Parm with Vodka Sauce, asparagus/eggplant "parm," spinach-mozzarella meatballs. This is the most consistent theme from 2019 to 2025. Evidence: CSV rows; [Pinterest](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/276901077078365141/), [dianemorrisey.com](http://dianemorrisey.com/category/dinner).
2. **Sheet-pan and one-pan shortcuts**: asparagus parm, Sheet Pan Lasagna, Sheet Pan Jambalaya (built "to use up leftover rice," [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/benefits-discounts/members-only-access/info-2025/sheet-pan-jambalaya-recipe-diane-morrisey.html)), Sheet Pan Breakfast Sammies.
3. **Smart store-bought shortcuts**: pizza dough (Butter Chicken Calzones, Christmas-tree bread), puff pastry, cake mix (Chocolate Streusel Coffee Cake), store-bought ravioli, Trader Joe's crust for a "clean out the fridge" pie ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/benefits-discounts/members-only-access/info-2025/diane-morrisey-you-got-this-cookbook.html)).
4. **Credited adaptations of other creators and magazines**: Southern Living (Marry Me Chicken Casserole), 1993 *Gourmet* (Nantucket Pie), Food52 (pomegranate carrots), @cooking__diaries_ (hasselback eggplant), @simplefood4you (crunchwrap). Rick Rodgers, the book's collaborator, notes she publishes the full recipe in her posts ([Substack](https://rickrodgers.substack.com/p/the-truth-about-influencer-cookbooks)).
5. **Vegetarian-forward but meat-inclusive**: she has not eaten meat in 25–30 years (sources differ; Daily Voice says pescatarian) but cooks meat for the family. The book is designed for "carnivores, pescatarians, and vegetarians alike." Sources: [Daily Voice](https://dailyvoice.com/connecticut/trumbull/lifestyle/move-over-martha-trumbulls-diane-morrisey-is-social-medias-rising-foodie-star/779507/), [AOL](https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/food-influencer-diane-morrisey-shares-130058322.html), [S&S](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/You-Got-This!/Diane-Morrisey/9781668033401).
6. **Big-family practicality**: doubling meatballs and freezing half ([pin](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/diane-morrisey-on-instagram-when-it-comes-to-meal-prep-for-my-family-something-that-ive-always-done-is-when-i-am-making-meatballs-i-double-the-recipe-i-u--371406300540137890/)), a 9x13 tray of 20 bars ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/home-living/strawberry-oat-bars-recipe-diane-morrissey/)), and make-ahead casseroles.
7. **Baking and breakfast**: scones, muffins, focaccia art, and pancakes appear on Feedfeed and dianemorrisey.com. Baking is the largest single category in the CSV (20 rows), and the book opens with "Breakfast is My Jam."

"Dinner in 30" is **not** a named series in any source. Crab bisque "in less than 30 minutes" and the 20-minute Best Baked Salmon show speed is a selling point, but it is not branded.

- **Thanksgiving**: Nantucket Pie (yearly since 1993), Cranberry Orange Oat Bars, make-ahead twice-baked potato casserole, and the Food Network mayo-turkey video.
- **Christmas**: Christmas-tree cheese bread (GMA in two consecutive years), baked brie with puff-pastry swirl, hasselback eggplant as a "vegetarian Christmas showstopper," and Holiday Squash and Apple Salad. **No Christmas Eve / Feast of the Seven Fishes content by Diane Morrisey was found.** Do not assume it exists; ask her.
- **St. Patrick's Day / Irish**: colcannon (Mar 2024), Full Irish Breakfast, Boozy Irish Cream Brownies (book), and her Belfast heritage (2025 medal). This is an under-used seasonal hook with a real personal story.
- **Valentine's**: heart-crust raspberry pie (Feedfeed).
- **Summer**: crab bisque ("Summer Therapy"), summer halibut, Summertime Ravioli, balsamic strawberry chicken, s'mores blondies, eggplant/zucchini wraps, and baked potato cups (July).

## 3. Audience signals
- **Who:** She describes a surprisingly young following ("young professionals and kids who are just out of college") and says her age is part of the appeal, per a Woman's World interview cited in [search results](https://www.aarp.org/benefits-discounts/members-only-access/info-2025/diane-morrisey-you-got-this-cookbook.html) **[1 source; the original Woman's World piece was not retrieved]**. AARP chose to feature her for its 50+ members. Moms are a clear segment: Cool Mom Eats (2022, 2024), Fairfield County Mom, and "Trumbull mom" in WFSB coverage. **There is no demographic data; Diane's Instagram Insights are the gap.**
- **Why they follow:** encouragement and patient explanations, per [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/benefits-discounts/members-only-access/info-2025/diane-morrisey-you-got-this-cookbook.html). Her line "95 percent confidence and 5 percent the ability to read a recipe" is quoted by a Goodreads reviewer ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/214151508-you-got-this)). Rick Rodgers credits community: she "or one of her daughters answers comments and questions" ([Substack](https://rickrodgers.substack.com/p/the-truth-about-influencer-cookbooks)).
- **Super-fans who blog her recipes:** Sly Rooster says most of the author's saved recipes now come from her ([Sly Rooster](https://slyrooster.com/tag/diane-morrisey/)). Leland Ave Studios calls the book "my new favorite cookbook" ([Leland Ave](https://www.lelandavestudios.com/2025/06/26/you-got-this-is-my-new-favorite-cookbook/)). Bayou Mama calls it family-friendly and both "weeknight and weekend friendly" ([Bayou Mama](https://bayoumama.net/category/book-reviews/)). Hello Good Cooking adapts her scampi ([HGC](https://www.hellogoodcooking.com/recipes/oven-baked-shrimp-scampi)).
- **Praise:** "Surefire keeper" for the asparagus parm ([Parade](https://parade.com/food/diane-morissey-asparagus-parmesan-recipe-review)), "clear and simple instructions" (Leland Ave), and "will find favor with new cooks" ([Library Journal](https://www.libraryjournal.com/review/you-got-this-recipes-anyone-can-make-and-everyone-will-love-1813985)).
- **Criticism (public, mild):** S'mores Blondies were "slightly over-the-top" sweet ([The Kitchn](https://www.thekitchn.com/smores-blondies-recipe-review-23425217)). Parade's tester said the asparagus recipe was missing seasoning. A library review called the book "a bit uneven" with "treasures." Goodreads averages about 3.8–3.9 out of 5, and one reader wished every recipe had a photo ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/214151508-you-got-this)). Galesburg coworkers had mixed reactions to the chocolate-dipped chips ([Galesburg](https://galesburglibrary.org/cooking-the-books-you-got-this-recipes-anyone-can-make-and-everyone-will-love-by-diane-morrisey/)).
- **What people ask for:** the specific substitution and scaling questions in her comments could not be retrieved, because Instagram and Reddit were not accessible. Indirect evidence is her own built-in swaps ("any combination of jam and berries" in the oat bars, the vegetarian main plus a simple meat side in the book intro, Beyond burgers in the crunchwrap). Treat "substitutions and scaling" as a **hypothesis to verify with her comment data**.
- **A finding that matters for the site:** the earliest coverage already shows people having trouble *finding* her recipes. One blog wrote that she "does not have a blog that I can link," and the Parade pieces point readers to her Instagram captions ([Parade](https://parade.com/food/hasselback-eggplant)). A dianemorrisey.com with a small, older recipe set (breakfast, dinner, pasta, appetizers, dessert categories) exists but appears dormant. **This is a sign that people want the recipes in one findable place.**

## 4. Consistency verdict

**Verdict: STABLE core, gradually widening.** From the 2019 baked ziti to the 2025 book, the center of gravity has been **approachable, family-scale, Italian-American-leaning comfort food with smart shortcuts**, framed by confidence ("You Got This," "Real Food for Real People"). What has changed over time:
- **Format** moved from photo posts to Reels in 2022, which drove growth, and from blog-style focaccia art (c. 2020) to fast video.
- **The vegetable/vegetarian share has grown.** The 2023 and 2025 viral peaks were both eggplant dishes, and the book explicitly serves vegetarians. This fits her own diet rather than marking a pivot.
- **Global flavors widened in the book** (butter chicken, gochujang, Thai salad, curry, ginger pork) next to the Italian core.
- **Baking has been constant**, but it gets less press than her savory recipes.

**Confidence: medium.** It is strong on themes, because the same patterns show up across about 20 independent outlets and 102 recipes. It is weak on quantities. Gaps: (a) no post-level save or share data; (b) dates are missing for about two-thirds of the recipes; (c) no comment text; (d) the Daily Voice and early CT Post dates are inferred; (e) TikTok and Facebook numbers are thin or undated; (f) the current 2M figure is undated.
