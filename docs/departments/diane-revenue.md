# Revenue & Partnerships: Diane Morrisey

## 1) Findings with sources
- **She already does paid brand work.** Marketers "paid her to create recipes using their products" ([CT Post](https://www.ctpost.com/news/article/trumbull-food-influencer-diane-morrisey-1m-follow-17862816.php)). Brands approached her first ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/entertainment/books/diane-morrisey-you-got-this-cookbook/)). Neither source names a brand, and we found no public #ad posts. So partnerships are an existing line to formalize, not a new one.
- **Sponsored-post rates at 1M+ followers:** most sources put this at $10K–25K per post in the US ([Creator Hero](https://www.creator-hero.com/blog/how-much-does-instagram-pay-for-1m-followers-in-2025)). Reels for 1M+ accounts run $11.25K–18.75K ([Collabed](https://collabed.app/rates/instagram-reels/1m-plus)), and mega creators run $10K to $100K+ ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-rates/instagram-influencer-rates/)). These are vendor sites, so treat them as directional. A recipe-development fee is typically charged separately and adds 30–50% ([Influencer Fee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/food-influencer-rates)).
- **Recipe development:** recipe-only work is $250–700+ per recipe ([Recipekit](https://recipekit.com/blogs/our-blog/recipe-development-vs-sponsored-posts-pricing-guide)), and Vegan Richa starts at $400 per recipe ([Vegan Richa](https://www.veganricha.com/advertising/)). A studio charges about $2K per recipe with a photo, and a 12-recipe program runs $20K–50K ([Heart Creative](https://heartcreative.co/food-beverage-content-cost/)). Our $2.5K–15K per project guess fits a 5–10 recipe project with her name and reach attached.
- **Online classes:** Milk Street livestreams cost $29.95 ([Milk Street](https://www.177milkstreet.com/milk-street-cooking-school/online-events)). Sur La Table charges from $39 **per household** ([Sur La Table](https://www.surlatable.com/cooking-classes/)).
- **In-person classes:** Sur La Table in Canton, CT starts at $69 per person ([Sur La Table CT](https://www.surlatable.com/locations/ct/canton/cooking-classes-167.html)). Other listings show $89 for in-store classes and $95 for private classes ([Sur La Table](https://www.surlatable.com/cooking-classes/)).
- **Corporate team building:** Home Cooking NY charges $155 per person with an 8-person minimum plus 15% gratuity ([Home Cooking NY](https://homecookingny.com/corporateevents/)). Cooking Class NY charges $149 per person with a $1,200 minimum ([Cooking Class NY](https://cookingclassny.com/team-building-cooking-class-nyc)). In Fairfield and Hartford County, Chef Silvia charges $150–175 per person plus a venue fee ([Chef Silvia](https://chefsilvia.com/corporate-cooking-classes/)). At 20–40 people that is $3K–6K. Reaching $10K needs a celebrity premium (est.).
- **Memberships:** NYT Cooking costs about $5.99 per month or $49.99 per year ([Zimmer](https://zimmerfood.com/articles/meal-planning-apps-comparison)). $5 Meal Plan costs $5 per month after a 14-day trial ([5dollarmealplan](https://5dollarmealplan.com)). Mealime shut down on Oct 21, 2026 ([Zimmer](https://zimmerfood.com/articles/meal-planning-apps-comparison)), which leaves a gap in the market. Caroline Chambers' *What to Cook* is $5 per month ([Clubb](https://www.theclubb.co/blog/top-25-the-most-successful-food-substacks)). A membership vendor says prices under $7 per month suffer from fee drag ([Member Kitchens](https://memberkitchens.com/updates/how-much-should-you-charge-for-a-meal-planning-membership)).
- **Cookbook:** the common hardcover royalty is 10% of list for the first 5,000 copies, 12.5% for the next 5,000, then 15% ([Sudowrite](https://sudowrite.com/blog/how-much-do-authors-really-make-2025-data-on-royalties-advances-and-income/)). Some contracts pay on net instead of list, which roughly halves the rate ([Barker Books](https://barkerbooks.com/royalties-for-books/)). On a $35 book that is $3.50–5.25 per copy, but royalties are paid only once the advance earns out. Cookbook advances range from about $10K to $100K ([HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/how-much-money-cookbook-authors-make_l_5e989ecac5b69933645e3d3c)).
- **FTC rules:** the influencer is responsible for disclosing any "material connection," including free product ([FTC Disclosures 101](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers/)). The 2023 Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255) require disclosures to be "difficult to miss" and made in the same medium as the endorsement, so on video both on screen and spoken. Disclosures hidden behind "more" do not count, and a platform's built-in tool alone "likely will not suffice" ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/07/26/2023-14795/guides-concerning-the-use-of-endorsements-and-testimonials-in-advertising); [InfoLawGroup](https://infolawgroup.com/insights/2023/7/31/ftc-releases-updated-endorsement-guides-10-key-takeaways)). Tagging a brand can itself count as an endorsement.
- **What an honest partnership requires (our standard):** (1) She uses the product before signing. (2) She gives a spoken plus on-screen "Paid partnership with X" in the first seconds and "#ad" at the start of the caption. (3) She makes no claims she can't verify. (4) She keeps editorial veto, so the brand cannot script her opinion. (5) Sponsored recipes stay free, like every other recipe.
