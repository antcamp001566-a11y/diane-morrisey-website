## 3) Research findings
- **Social reach is rented.** Instagram average reach is ~3.5% per post, down ~12% year over year ([SocialInsider via Benchmark](https://www.benchmarkemail.com/blog/organic-social-reach-decline-email-marketing/)). Other analyses put publishers at 2–3% ([Blog Herald](https://blogherald.com/social-media/why-organic-reach-on-instagram-has-collapsed-for-publishers-in-2025/)). These are vendor sources, so treat the figures as directional. An email list is portable, and no algorithm decides who sees it ([Nutshell](https://www.nutshell.com/blog/email-marketing-vs-social-media)).
- **Ad tolerance.** The Coalition for Better Ads tested 104 formats with more than 25,000 consumers. Pop-ups, ad density over 30%, autoplay video with sound and large sticky ads were among the least liked on mobile ([IAB](https://www.iab.com/news/coalition-better-ads-releases-initial-better-ads-standards-desktop-mobile-web-north-america-europe), [Google](https://support.google.com/webtools/answer/7073774)). 58% call pop-ups the most annoying ad type ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/worst-ads-poll-survey)). Long intros make room for more ad slots, and a "jump to recipe" button skips the intro but still leaves the ads ([Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/blog/the-dos-and-donts-of-monetizing-a-recipe-site-or-food-blog), [RecipeStripper](https://recipestripper.com/blog/how-to-get-just-the-recipe)). We found **no NN/g recipe-site study**, so these are secondary sources.
- **Search.** Google reads Recipe markup (ingredients, times, images) in JSON-LD ([Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/recipe)). Adults use search engines, YouTube and Instagram to find recipes ([UCA study](https://research.uca.ac.uk/6608/1/DHocaoglu_EFOOD_2024.pdf)). Google has promoted "substitute for buttermilk" as a use case ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/search/thanksgiving-fun-with-google/)), but **we found no trend data on substitution searches.** Our proxy is her own comment log (D4) plus [Phase 1: P__ @ mm:ss].
- **Pew:** we did not find a primary Pew recipe stat. Do not cite the secondhand figure.

## 4) What this changes
- **D1:** each recipe needs the 2-minute card at the top, swaps, Recipe schema, and filters by tool and pantry. Feel Good Foodie shows that an IG-captions-to-site move works.
- **D2/D3:** a Reel's job is to move viewers to the site and the email list. With ~3% reach, the list is the one asset Diane owns.
- **D5:** adopt a "clean page" rule (M2) before any ad deal, because Natasha's readers complain openly about ads.
- **D7:** do not claim "NYT bestseller" in the media kit until we verify the list week (M4). Use dated follower counts.
- **D9:** BudgetBytes+ and Mel's show that an ad-free meal-plan tier is a proven model. Keep it optional and easy to cancel.
- **Ask Diane agent:** feeds M1's swap boxes.
