# 00 — The Intent Standard

> **AI-assisted document.** Drafted with Claude (AI) as an evaluation lens. Per MK-320 rules, the team develops its recommendations first, then uses this to check them. Label any part used in the deck or action plan as AI-assisted.

Every other document in this folder is written against this standard. It exists because the most common way a personal website goes wrong isn't malice. It's **intent drift**: a site built to serve an audience slowly turns into a site built to collect from that audience, and nobody decides that on purpose.

The question is never only "will this make money?" It's **"what is this *for*?"**

---

## The Intent Spectrum

| Level | Name | The question it started from | Role of the audience | Role of revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Revenue-first** | "How does Diane make money from this site?" | Source of money | The purpose |
| 2 | **Revenue-led** | "How do we monetize the people who visit?" | A market | Primary goal; value is the means |
| 3 | **Exchange** | "What can Diane offer at a fair price?" | Trading partner | Equal to value |
| 4 | **Value-led** | "What does her audience need that Diane can sustainably provide?" | The reason | Viability check |
| 5 | **Value-first** | "What would make her audience's lives better, and keep them coming back?" | The point | Fuel and byproduct |

**Target: every recommendation sits at level 4 or 5.** Revenue isn't forbidden. It's a *constraint the idea has to meet*, not *the reason the idea exists*.

## Why intent matters even when nothing harmful happens

Intent decides three things, and they compound:

1. **What gets measured.** A revenue-first site tracks conversions and clicks. A value-first site tracks return visits, time spent and whether people share it.
2. **Where the investment goes.** Revenue-first effort goes into *capture*: pop-ups, gates, funnels, ad slots. Value-first effort goes into *the thing itself*: the writing, the stories, the usefulness.
3. **What improves over time.** You get good at whatever you optimize. Optimize capture, and you end up with an efficient way to collect from content nobody's attached to.

## Diagnosing intent (it's invisible, so look for signals)

| Signal | Value-first (4–5) | Revenue-first (1–2) |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin trace**: where did the idea come from? | Something the audience said in a Phase 1 interview | A revenue goal or a tactic copied from another site |
| **Success metric** | Return visits, replies, shares, time spent | Clicks, conversions, revenue only |
| **Grammar test**: who is the subject? | "Give readers a reason to come back every week" | "Get visitors to sign up / buy" |
| **Swap test**: value with no money vs. money with no value | Diane would pick value | Diane would pick money |
| **Investment destination** | Content, experience, relationship | Conversion mechanics |

## Re-rooting

Don't throw out level 1–2 ideas. **Re-root** them: keep the outcome and change the starting question.

| Revenue-first | Re-rooted |
|---|---|
| "Add an email pop-up to grow the list" | "What would readers want delivered to them so they don't miss it?" Then offer exactly that. |
| "Add affiliate links to monetize" | "What does her audience ask her to recommend?" Recommend only that, disclosed. |
| "Sell a course" | "What do people repeatedly ask Diane to teach or explain?" |

## The rule for every recommendation

1. **Label** its intent level using the signals above.
2. **Re-root** anything at level 1–2 before Diane sees it.
3. **Name the value investment**, the part of the budget that goes into the thing itself. That's the part a profit focus will be tempted to cut.
4. **Then** show the revenue math, as proof that the value-first version is viable.

> **"Revenue is how you'll know it worked, not what you're trying to do."**

## Tags used in this folder

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[Suggested]` | An industry-standard recommendation, not derived from the repo or interviews. Review before using. |
