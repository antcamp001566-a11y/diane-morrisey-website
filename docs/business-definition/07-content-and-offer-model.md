# 07 — Content & Offer Model: Free, Book, Services

> **AI-assisted document.** Defines what Diane gives away, what the cookbook is *for*, and what she sells. Dollar figures are `[Suggested]` estimates benchmarked in the [Revenue dept. report](../departments/diane-revenue.md); confirm with `[Ask Diane]`.

## The problem

Diane has three things competing with each other:

1. **Free content** (Reels, captions, soon the website)
2. **The cookbook** (*You Got This!*)
3. **Everything else she could sell**

Without clear rules, they cannibalize each other. **Every Reel that gives away a cookbook recipe in full is a reason not to buy the book.** And if the book is the only thing for sale, every free post works against her income.

## The position: the cookbook is a relationship builder, not the revenue engine

**Why:**
- **The math is small.** The common hardcover royalty is **10% of list for the first 5,000 copies, 12.5% for the next 5,000, then 15%**; contracts paid on net roughly halve that ([Revenue dept.](../departments/diane-revenue.md)) `[Ask Diane for her actual contract]`. On a ~$35 book, that's roughly **$3.50–$5.25 a copy**, and nothing beyond the advance until it earns out. The Year-1 estimate therefore counts **$0** in royalties. Even a strong-selling cookbook is modest income next to services.
- **The book's real value is what it signals and starts.** Simon & Schuster credibility; a physical object in a follower's kitchen; proof that "Diane is real and her recipes work." It turns a follower into someone who has *invested* in her.
- **So the book should be measured as a relationship tool:** how many readers then join the email list, take a class or come to an event. Copies sold matter less.

**Implication:** cannibalization of *book sales* matters less than people assume. What matters is that **every piece of content has a defined job** and leads people one step deeper.

## The three content buckets

| Bucket | What's in it | Job | Where it lives | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Open** | Weeknight recipes, Reels, the site library, the newsletter | Earn trust; reach new cooks; bring people back | Instagram, website, email | 5 |
| **2. Book** | Recipes **original to the cookbook**, organized and tested as a collection, with her story and kitchen notes | Deepen the relationship: "I own a piece of Diane's kitchen" | *You Got This!*, future books | 4–5 |
| **3. Service** | Diane's **time, guidance and presence**: teaching, planning, events, expertise | **Revenue.** Things that can't be copied from a caption. | Classes, events, memberships, consulting | 4–5 |

### Rules that keep the buckets from cannibalizing each other `[Suggested]`

1. **Book recipes are not posted in full for free.** A Reel can *show* a book recipe being made: "This is page 112 of *You Got This!*." The written recipe stays in the book. The video sells the book instead of replacing it.
2. **Open recipes are never pulled behind a paywall.** Anything that has been free stays free. Taking it back breaks trust. `Intent: 1` if broken.
3. **Services sell what content can't:** Diane's live attention, personalization, planning and community. Never "the same recipe, but paid."
4. **Every bucket points one step deeper.** Reel → site recipe → email list → book → class or event. **No step is a trap**; each is useful on its own.
5. **The book contains doorways to services:** QR codes to technique videos on the site, a "cook with me" class invite, and a newsletter link. Build this into Cookbook #2 from day one.
6. **Sponsored recipes stay free**, like every other recipe, and every partnership is disclosed under the written FTC policy (card R1). On video the disclosure must be **spoken and shown on screen** in the first seconds, with "#ad" at the start of the caption; a tag hidden behind "more" or a platform's built-in label alone doesn't count (FTC Endorsement Guides, via [Revenue dept.](../departments/diane-revenue.md)).

### Why #1 is recipe development and consulting
It's the **one thing nobody else in her follower bracket can offer**: a food creator who also ran prepared foods for a major grocer. It pays the most per hour, doesn't depend on Instagram reach, and doesn't compete with her free content or her book at all. Brands already pay her for recipes, so this line is formalized, not invented. Don't put "NYT bestseller" in the media kit until the list week is verified (card M4, [Market dept.](../departments/diane-market.md)).

## Metrics per bucket

| Bucket | Measure | Don't over-index on |
|---|---|---|
| Open | Returning visitors, saves, "I made it" | Viral one-offs |
| Book | Readers → email sign-ups, class sign-ups (QR scans) | Copies sold alone |
| Services | Revenue, repeat customers, waitlists | Revenue that comes with falling trust metrics |

## Intent check

- Free stays free; the book is a gift-plus; services sell time and expertise. **All level 4–5.**
- Watch-out: if Reels start being cut short to "force" book sales ("recipe in the book!" on everything), Open content stops doing its job. **Keep most Reels complete. Tease the book only for book recipes.**
