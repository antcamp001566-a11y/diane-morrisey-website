> **The key intent conversation with Diane:** with ~2M followers, ad networks will pitch her. That's the biggest fork in this business. Option A is maximum ad revenue, which means the cluttered pages her followers complain about elsewhere. Option B is a fast, clean site funded mainly by books, classes and honest partnerships, possibly with light ads later. This document recommends **B**.

## Pricing philosophy

1. **Recipes are free, forever.** They're what built her audience.
2. **Charge for time saved, time together, and depth**: meal plans, classes, books.
3. **Price from value**, benchmarked against comparable creators (doc 04).
4. **One new paid offer at a time.**

## Roadmap `[Suggested]`

### Now (0–3 months): give the recipes a home
- [ ] Platform: WordPress + WP Recipe Maker (schema.org/Recipe for Google rich results, print and "cook mode" built in) on managed hosting. Or a static site such as Astro + Recipe JSON-LD if a developer maintains it.
- [ ] Migrate the **top 50 most-loved recipes** first, by Instagram saves and comments.

- [ ] Change the Instagram link in bio to the site; every new Reel says "full recipe on my site"

- **Exit metric:** steady Instagram→site traffic; returning visitors growing

### Next (3–9 months): build the relationship
- [ ] Launch **Ask Diane** on the top 50 recipes (doc 08)
- [ ] Free What's for Dinner newsletter
- [ ] Migrate the remaining back catalog (VA or intern)
- [ ] Search filters; holiday menu collections
- [ ] Work with Diane page + media kit
- **Exit metric:** newsletter list growing; readers replying "I made this!"

### Later (9+ months): let revenue follow
- [ ] Classes and events
- [ ] Cookbook #2, informed by site data
- [ ] Optional meal-plan tier, only if the trigger is met
- [ ] Ad decision (see above), only after deciding the rules
- **Exit metric:** revenue grows *without* returning visitors, saves or replies falling
