## The landscape

A personal website doesn't compete for *market share*. It competes for **attention and return visits**. Its real competitors are the other places Diane's audience could spend that attention, including doing nothing.

## Competitor categories (alternatives the audience already uses)

| Category | Examples | What they do well | Where a personal site wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Social media profiles** | Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok | Reach; where the audience already is | Diane owns it. No algorithm decides who sees her work, and content doesn't disappear in a feed. |
| **Newsletter platforms** | Substack, Beehiiv, Ghost | Direct relationship; built-in subscriptions | A full home: background, archive and contact in one place. A newsletter can be *part* of the site. |
| **Link-in-bio pages** | Linktree, Beacons | Fast and free | Depth. A link list isn't a reason to come back. |
| **Peer personal sites** | `[Team research]` Others in Diane's field | Show what the audience expects | `[Team research]` The gap they leave open. |
| **Doing nothing / word of mouth** | Status quo | No cost, personal | Scale and permanence. Her story keeps working when she isn't in the room. |

## Differentiation `[Suggested]`

1. **Built from her audience's own words.** Phase 1 interviews mean the content answers what people actually asked for. Most personal sites guess.
2. **Designed for the return visit**, not the first click. The content core and publishing rhythm are the product.
3. **Owned, not rented.** Social platforms can change the rules; the site and email list belong to Diane.
4. **Trust as strategy.** No manipulative tactics. That stands out as a positioning choice, not just ethics.
