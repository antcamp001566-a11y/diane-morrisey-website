## The landscape

Food creators with big social followings usually move to an **owned recipe website + newsletter**. It's the only asset the platforms can't take away, and search engines reward it with recipe rich results. Diane is unusual: a large audience and a major-publisher cookbook, but **no recipe site yet**. The opportunity is big, and so is the risk of building it the ad-heavy way.

## Competitor categories

| Category | Examples `[Team research]` | Strength | Diane's opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Creator recipe sites** | Smitten Kitchen, Once Upon a Chef, Half Baked Harvest, Dan Pelosi / Grossy Pelosi (who blurbed her book) | Owned home, search traffic, newsletters | Her family-of-six credibility and the "anyone can make" promise |
| **Ad-supported recipe giants** | Allrecipes, Food Network, Delish | Huge libraries, SEO dominance | Trust and a personal voice. Readers are tired of ad clutter. |
| **Paid recipe subscriptions** | NYT Cooking | Quality, organization, meal planning | Free core recipes from a person they already follow |
| **Newsletter-first creators** | Food Substacks | Direct relationship | A searchable library, not just an inbox |
| **Staying on Instagram** | Diane's current setup | Free; where her audience already is | Search, print, ownership, and not depending on the algorithm |

## Differentiation

1. **The trust she already has.** Mom of six, former Whole Foods prepared-foods executive, Simon & Schuster author, about 2M followers.
2. **Built to get you to the food.** A deliberate contrast with ad-heavy recipe sites.
3. **One-tap bridge from Instagram.** Every Reel links straight to its recipe card.
4. **Owned, not rented.** The site and email list stay hers if Instagram changes.
